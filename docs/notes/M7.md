# M7: the public site

Started 2026-11-09, alongside M8b, in `site/` and `docs/` only.

## Panels, in the order decided (2026-11-09)

1. **The headline.** Le Verrier's data → the planet's longitude on Galle's night against
   Neptune's, with mass and distance.
2. **What the data measured.** The 1821 conjunction figure.
3. **The lucky window,** 1800–1870.
4. **Honesty.** The pre-registration timeline, the repeatability correction and the
   sensitivity runs.
5. **The dataset,** with its DOIs and citation.

This order replaces the spec's five-panel list (PHASE1_SPEC, M7) and DESIGN.md's four scenes as
the content plan. DESIGN.md still sets the visual language. Panels 1–3 are built (2026-11-09);
panels 4 and 5 wait for review of panel 3.

## Data contract

- Every number and figure is read from `site/data/*.json` at load time (`site/js/data.js`).
  No science value is written in the site's code; the only numeric literals are display
  constants (scale, curve smoothing) and the Julian-date-to-calendar algorithm.
- So M8b and the arbiter update the site when their JSON lands, with no code change.
- **Pending results come from the JSON's status:**
  - 95 % interval: `m6c_replacement.json` lists its bounds under `provisional`;
    `m8_runs.json` `rule_95.replace` is false; `m8b.json` is absent until M8b reports.
    - Absent → "The 95 % range, X, is provisional; a final check is still running."
    - Present with `rule_95.replace` true → M8b's interval, shown as final.
    - Present and false → "provisional", with the failed conditions named.
  - The S1 verdict belongs to panel 4 and will read `m8_arbiter.json` the same way.
- A missing optional file is fetched and gets a 404. The browser logs it as a console error.
  For `m8b.json` this is the pending signal and goes away once M8b writes the file.

## Panel 1: the headline (`site/js/scene-headline.js`)

**Sources**

| shown | from |
|---|---|
| median, 68 % and 95 % range | `m6c_replacement.json` `galle_lon_ecl_of_date_deg` |
| Neptune's longitude (JPL) and Le Verrier's own (derived from his elements) | the same block, `vs.jpl.target`, `vs.le_verrier_derived.target` |
| posterior curve, cone footprint, hover | `m6c_conjunction.json` `histograms.galle_night_geo`. The loader checks it is centred on the same median |
| mass and semi-major axis | `m6c_replacement.json` `mass_earth`, `a_au` |
| Neptune's mass and semi-major axis | `m5a_fits.json` `true_neptune` (`a_au_osculating_1781`, the same 1781 epoch as the fit) |
| Galle's night | `m6c_conjunction.json` `galle_night_jd_tdb` |
| measurement count | `m6c_replacement.json` `best_fit.n_measurements` |
| observation count | `m6_fits.json` `description`, parsed ("279 observations, 529 measurements"). The loader checks the 529 against `best_fit.n_measurements` and stops if they differ. A structured field would be better; it needs an export change in `src/`, left for later |
| Le Verrier's printed prediction and its source (hover) | `m6_fits.json` `comparison_targets.le_verrier`: `helio_lon_ecl_of_date_1847_deg` (shown as 326°32′) and `sources[0]`. The epoch label "1 Jan 1847" is text, tied to that key |
| 95 % status | `m6c_replacement.json` `provisional`, `m8_runs.json` and `m8b.json` `rule_95` |

**Geometry**
- The ecliptic grid runs to a glowing horizon.
- The posterior stands on the grid as a lit curve.
  - For display only, the curve is smoothed with a Gaussian of σ = 0.5° (2 bins). The text
    alternative says so.
  - The cone, the CDF and the hover use the raw 0.25° counts.
- A cone of 17 lines falls from the inferred planet (a shaded sphere with a dashed halo,
  because it is inferred). The lines land on the posterior's quantiles 16 %, …, 84 %, so the
  cone's footprint is the 68 % interval.
- Neptune is a diamond on a solid hairline (solid means true). Le Verrier's own orbit is dashed
  (dashed means predicted). Its label says the 325.0° is our calculation from his published
  elements.
- Every series is labelled directly, so there is no legend.
- The 95 % range is a sentence in the body, worded from its status (pending, provisional after
  M8b, or final).

**Interaction**
- Drag orbits the camera slightly.
- Hovering the curve shows a longitude and the share of the posterior below it.
- Hovering Le Verrier's line or label shows his printed prediction, 326°32′ heliocentric for
  1 Jan 1847, with its source.
- The curve and then the cone draw in on load, except under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

**Accessibility**
- The canvas and the overlay are `aria-hidden`.
- The text alternative is generated from the same data and is in the DOM.
- The text alternative also carries Le Verrier's printed prediction and the 95 % sentence, so
  neither is hover-only. The equation carries an `aria-label`.

**Phone**
- The copy takes the top third, and the image is shifted down below it.
- Neptune's label stays anchored, and the dashed line keeps a short "Le Verrier" label (full
  label and subtitle on desktop only). The median label is hidden. Mass and distance move to a
  block at the bottom.
- The equation and axis title are hidden.

**Checked**
- Headless Chrome (SwiftShader WebGL) at 1440 × 900 and 390 × 844.
- Hover tooltip, drag and reduced motion.
- No page errors. The one console error is the `m8b.json` 404, the pending signal above.
- Screenshots: `docs/notes/M7_headline.jpg` and `docs/notes/M7_headline_phone.jpg`.
- Some scripted loads timed out without reaching "ready", with no page error. Immediate reruns
  passed twice. The likely cause is the CDN fetch of three.js; this is not confirmed.
- Lighthouse was not run yet. It is an acceptance item for the finished site.

**Libraries:** three.js 0.169.0 from jsDelivr, through an import map. Fonts are Barlow
Condensed and EB Garamond, from Google Fonts.

### Self-audit, panel 1 (UI rule; revised 2026-11-09)

Revision: the readout block, the legend, the vertical edge title and the "01 ·" numbering are
gone. The 95 % line moved into the body.

Patterns from the list that panel 1 uses, and why:
- **ALL CAPS labels:** used only for the annotation titles, the axis title and the eyebrow. This
  is the pilot-manual annotation language in DESIGN.md, and it sets labels apart from prose.
  Headline and body are sentence case.
- **Eyebrow** ("Where to look"): names the panel's question in a site of five one-screen
  panels. No section number.
- **Gradient:** the horizon glow, the scene's light source, and the sphere's shading. Because a
  gradient raises the bar, the reference's registration marks and star field are left out.
- **Grain** (a dithering-like texture) at 0.10 opacity: it breaks up 8-bit banding in the glow
  on dark screens.
- **Several opacities of one white:** these are the hierarchy in a greyscale-only design. Grid
  0.17, cone 0.4, Le Verrier's line 0.85, curve 1. Text uses the four ink tokens.
- **Italics:** only in the equation, as mathematical convention.
Not used: section numbering, a legend, a readout block, the vertical edge title, tags or pills, Instrument Serif, negative kerning, dividers, bordered containers (the
tooltip has a background and no border), ASCII images, squircles, monospace (tabular numerals
come from Barlow), periods in titles, code breadcrumbs (the loading line no longer names
`site/data/`). Detail is kept to four annotations, each tied to a number.

## Panel 2: what the data measured (`site/js/scene-conjunction.js`)

Built 2026-11-09 from `m6c_conjunction.json` alone: the after-the-fact diagnostic in M6.md
("the planet at the 1821 conjunction"). The panel says it is after the fact, using the JSON's
own description.

**Sources** (all `m6c_conjunction.json`)

| shown | from |
|---|---|
| conjunction date; JPL's own Uranus–Neptune conjunction (Neptune's label) | `epoch.jd_tdb`, `epoch.jpl_uranus_neptune_conjunction_jd_tdb` |
| Galle's night | `galle_night_jd_tdb` |
| the two cards' histograms, per group | `histograms.conjunction_helio`, `histograms.galle_night_geo`. The loader checks each is centred on its epoch's median |
| 68 % widths, medians, ranges (body, labels, hover) | `conjunction_helio.all`, `galle_night_geo.all` |
| "seen from the Sun" width on Galle's night | `galle_night_helio.all.width68` |
| branch names, shares, branch separation | `groups`, `conjunction_helio.groups.*.share`, `|outer.median − inner.median|` per epoch |
| Neptune and Le Verrier per epoch, and where Le Verrier falls | `*.jpl_neptune`, `*.le_verrier` (`minus_median_deg`, `in_68`, `in_95`) |
| frames (hover) | `frames` |
| "after-the-fact" note | `description`, up to its colon |

**Geometry** (DESIGN.md "time as layers")
- Two translucent cards stand on a floor slab: 1821 in front, 1846 behind.
- Each card carries the core and the two eccentric branches as step histograms, 0.25° bins,
  each group scaled to unit area as in `M6c_conjunction.png`. The core is solid 2px; the
  branches are 1.2px at lower brightness.
  - The histograms are not smoothed, because smoothing would blur the width comparison the
    panel exists to make.
  - The other cells (2.3 %) are not drawn. The text alternative says so.
- The x axis is degrees from each card's median, the same scale on both cards. The camera is
  orthographic, so the two widths compare directly, with no perspective shrink.
- A ribbon on the floor joins the 1821 68 % range to the 1846 one. Hairlines join each group's
  median across the 25 years, so the branches visibly fan out.
- Le Verrier's orbit is dashed on both cards (our calculation, as in panel 1). Neptune is the
  diamond.
- Equation: δλ(t) ≈ −(3/2) n (δa/a)(t − t_c). A spread in a becomes a spread in mean motion,
  which grows with time since the conjunction. This is why the 1846 range is wider.

**Interaction:** hovering a card highlights it and shows that epoch's numbers. Drag orbits the
view slightly. The ribbon draws back from 1821 to 1846 when the panel first scrolls into view,
except under reduced motion. A panel renders only while it is on screen.

**Phone:** the view zooms out below the copy. The card labels pin to the cards. The branches
keep short "Outer" and "Inner" labels (no subtitle). The Neptune and ribbon labels are hidden;
the body and text alternative carry their numbers.

**Revision (2026-11-09):** the body no longer says the Galle-night width is 4.17° seen from the
Sun. That check (the change of viewpoint does not explain the widening) stays in the text
alternative and the 1846 card's hover, and in M6.md (the conjunction section, which records it).

**Page:** the panels are full-viewport sections with scroll snapping.
- Each panel loads its own data and shows its own error if loading fails.
- `loadJSON` fetches each file once per page and retries network errors twice.
  - The reason: with eight parallel requests, Python's `http.server` (a listen backlog of 5)
    reset connections on macOS. That was the cause of the intermittent "never ready" loads
    noted under panel 1.
  - After the change: five consecutive clean loads.

**Checked:**
- 1440 × 900 and 390 × 844 in headless Chrome (SwiftShader).
- Hover on both cards.
- No page errors. The only console error is the `m8b.json` 404 (pending).
- No horizontal overflow on the phone.
- Screenshots: `docs/notes/M7_conjunction.jpg` and `docs/notes/M7_conjunction_phone.jpg`.

## Panel 3: the lucky window (`site/js/scene-window.js`)

Built 2026-11-09 from `m6c_offset_track.json` alone: the after-the-fact diagnostic in M6.md
("Le Verrier's planet against the posterior, 1800–1870"). His published elements are held fixed
and carried through time; the panel does not ask what he would have predicted with less data,
and it says the search fields are not modelled.

**Sources** (all `m6c_offset_track.json`)

| shown | from |
|---|---|
| the channel: 68 % and 95 % bands about the median, every quarter-year | `series.year`, `lo95_rel_deg`, `lo68_rel_deg`, `hi68_rel_deg`, `hi95_rel_deg` (the loader checks all series have one length) |
| Le Verrier's path (dashed), Neptune's (solid) | `series.le_verrier_minus_median_deg`, `series.jpl_minus_median_deg` |
| zero crossing, 1842.09 | `le_verrier_zero_crossings_year` |
| posts at 1836, Galle's night, 1856 (dates, Le Verrier − Neptune, band, half-widths) | `marks.search_1836`, `marks.galle_night`, `marks.search_1856` |
| the window bar, 1835.75–1856.50 (inside the 68 %), and 1830.50 on (inside the 95 %, text alternative) | computed in the loader from the series: runs of samples where Le Verrier lies inside the band, at the series' quarter-year resolution |
| "21-year window", and whether Galle's night is in it | the window's length, rounded; the headline falls back to a neutral title if there is not exactly one window or Galle's night is outside it |
| "within 0.2° of the median" (Neptune's label) | max of `|jpl_minus_median_deg|` |
| frame, after-the-fact note | `frame`, `description` up to its colon |

**Geometry** (DESIGN.md: "the drift is the gap between two paths")
- The channel lies on the floor: time left to right, offset from the median into depth (ahead
  of the median at the back). 68 % is the brighter strip, 95 % the fainter one, each with an
  edge hairline; the median is a hairline.
- Le Verrier's path is dashed (predicted) and draws in from 1800 when the panel first scrolls
  into view. Neptune's is solid (true) and sits on the median.
- Posts rise from his planet's place at 1836, the zero crossing, Galle's night and 1856. A faint
  gate stands across the 95 % band at each.
- A bar in front marks the years his planet lies inside the 68 % band.
- Orthographic camera, so band widths read true across the 70 years.
- Equation: Δλ(t) ≈ (n_LV − n)(t − t₀), n = √(GM/a³). His orbit is wider (a = 36.15 AU), so it
  is slower and falls behind; the band widens for the reason in panel 2.

**Interaction:** hovering the floor draws a probe across the channel at that year and shows the
68 % and 95 % bands there, Le Verrier's offset and where it falls, and Neptune's. Drag orbits
slightly. Reduced motion skips the draw-in.

**Phone:** 1800–1870 is fitted to the width, and the offset axis is stretched 2.4× so the
channel keeps its height (the hover undoes the stretch). Labels shorten to "23 Sep 1836",
"1842.09", "Galle", "23 Sep 1856", "Le Verrier", "Neptune" and "68 % · 1835.75–1856.50". The 1856
post is short at phone scale and its top lands on Neptune's line, so on a phone that label
attaches to the post's foot, on Le Verrier's dashed path, and sits below it. The band-end and
ahead/behind labels and the equation are hidden. The dashes are longer, so they stay dashes at
the smaller scale.

**Checked:** 1440 × 900 and 390 × 844, hover, no page errors (only the `m8b.json` 404), no
horizontal overflow on the phone. Screenshots: `docs/notes/M7_window.jpg` and
`docs/notes/M7_window_phone.jpg`.

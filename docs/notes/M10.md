## Pre-registration (written 2026-11-10, before any M10 code or run)

the 19 observations before 1781 (data set A constrained, B flat; `docs/notes/M9.md`). That
profile used the headline's Gaussian error model. Gaussian tails let a few old measurements

pre-1781 observers carry it on his own?

### What changes

**1. The likelihood: Student-t errors, ν = 4, S3's scales.** Each measurement i in group g:
r_i − b_g ~ t_ν(scale s_g) with ν = 4 fixed, as in M6c's S3 (`docs/notes/M6.md`, "S3:
Student-t errors"; `src/fork/model/student_t.py`). The scales s_g are S3's: the ν = 4
maximum-likelihood scales of O − JPL per group, the `sigma_t4` column of the frozen headline
error model:

| group | ancient RA / Dec | Greenwich RA / Dec | Paris 1801–1827 RA / Dec | Paris 1835–1845 RA / Dec |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| s_g (″) | 4.796 / 3.584 | 1.784 / 2.095 | 2.217 / 2.184 | 1.366 / 1.076 |

The offsets keep their Gaussian prior, b_g ~ N(0, τ_g²), τ = 2″ modern, 5″ ancient.

**The profiled quantity** is ln L(a) = −½ Q_min(a), with

  Q(θ, u, b) = Σ_i (ν + 1) ln(1 + (r_i − b_g(i))² / (ν s_g(i)²)) + Σ_g (b_g / τ_g)²,

minimised over the perturber θ (a held), Uranus's four in-plane elements u and the eight
offsets b. Q is −2 ln L up to a constant that does not depend on any parameter (the t density's
normalisation and Σ ln s_g), so Δ ln L(a) is exact.
- The offsets are minimised, not integrated out, as in M9. Under Gaussian errors the two differ
  by a constant; under Student-t they do not, so **S3's marginal** (`StudentTObservations.chi2`,
  the normalised −2 ln L with every offset integrated by 48-node Gauss–Hermite quadrature) **is
  evaluated at every polished optimum and reported beside Q**. Its curve, relative to its own
  maximum, is given with the profile's. The reading rule uses Q. If the difference
  (marginal − Q) varies by more than 1.0 across a data set's grid (0.5 in ln L, a quarter of
  the −2 threshold), the report says so and gives the reading of both curves; the Q reading
  stands as the pre-registered one.
- For ν → ∞ with s_g → σ_g, Q becomes M9's augmented χ². `tests/test_m10.py` checks this limit
  against M9's exact objective at M9's a = 30 AU optimum (data set A), and checks Q against
  `t_marginal_m2lnl`'s data term at the IRLS offsets.

**2. The optimiser.** `scipy.optimize.least_squares` (trust-region reflective, bounds, the
e ≤ 0.3 disk by `fit._fit_bounded`'s two-phase guard, (i, Ω) riding along, as M9) with the
**Student-t loss** ρ(z) = (ν + 1) ln(1 + z/ν) on the scaled residuals f_i = (r_i − b̂_g)/s_g,
so that scipy's cost ½ Σ ρ(f_i²) is ½ × the data part of Q.
- At each evaluation the offsets b̂ are solved by iteratively reweighted least squares
  (`student_t.t_offset_modes`, the EM fixed point, started from the Gaussian solution), and
  each group's prior term enters as one pseudo-residual f_g with ρ(f_g²) = (b̂_g/τ_g)², i.e.
  f_g² = ν (exp((b̂_g/τ_g)² / (ν + 1)) − 1). The cost is then exactly ½ Q(θ, u, b̂), and b̂ is
  a stationary point of Q, so the gradient through b̂ vanishes (envelope theorem).
- **Variables: ten.** M9 projected Uranus out of the whitened vector (the linear Gaussian
  marginal); the Student-t objective has no such closed form, so Uranus's four in-plane
  elements are optimiser variables in both the search and the exact polish. Random starts take
  Uranus at u_ref; posterior starts take their sample's Uranus; continuation carries all ten.
- **Convergence, pre-registered now (M9 added it after the fact):** a polish counts as
  converged when scipy's status is positive and Q changed by less than 0.01 over its last five
  iterations. One that is not is restarted from its end point, up to four rounds; every round
  is reported with its status, final gradient norm and last-five change. A polish still not
  converged after four rounds is reported as such and its point is treated as failed
  ("Rerun on failure" above).

**3. The search model.** The M8 first-order perturbative model gives Uranus's position change
for the perturber (`PerturbativeProblem.delta_x`, `second_order=False`, Δs = 60 d, cubic
quadrature, about u_ref from `M8_u_ref.json`, as M8 and M9). Uranus's own four elements enter
linearly about u_ref, through the same Jacobian the Gaussian marginal uses but in arcsec, not
whitened: r(θ, u) = r_ref(θ) + J_u (u − u_ref). The search minimises Q on that residual.
`PerturbativeProblem.build` refuses Student-t observations (`TypeError`, M8's scope); M10
builds it on the Gaussian `RaDecObservations` and uses only its geometry, response and
Jacobian, never its Gaussian marginal. The exact polish does not depend on u_ref or on the
linearisation.

### Pre-run code changes (each committed before the launch, hash recorded here)

**(a) B's Uranus-only box re-centred.** M9 found that B's Uranus box, built by
`uranus_box_radec` on the post-1781 rows, is centred on a poor local solution of the
Uranus-only fit (χ² 2,900 for 488 degrees of freedom; with the same perturber, Uranus at the
box centre gives χ² 68,712 against 587 at the optimum), so every B point sat 1.6–1.85
half-widths outside it.
- **Method:** the Uranus-only fit is started twice, from the data-only line start it uses now
  (`initial_uranus_theta_radec`, docs/RULES.md rule 2) and from the headline's u_ref
  (`M8_u_ref.json`, itself a fit to the data alone); the box is centred on the solution with the
  lower χ², with the same half-width rule (n_sigma × the formal σ scaled by √(χ²/dof)). Both
  χ² are recorded in the box and reported. The leak test is unchanged: neither start reads
  Neptune.
- **Scope:** the box enters only the Uranus-box flag ("u box" in the tables) and the
  perturbative build (its finite-difference steps and volume term), never the exact objective
  that is read. For data set A the two starts are expected to meet; if they do not, the lower
  wins and the difference is reported. The headline's box (M6c) is not rebuilt; M9's results are
  not re-read.
- The new box applies to all six data sets.

**(b) A cache-only ephemeris fetcher.** `fork.data.horizons.state_vectors_ecl_j2000` fetches
any epoch missing from `data/cache/` from Horizons, live. A pre-registered run must not do that
silently. The change: a `cache_only` fetcher that raises on a miss, naming the body and the
missing epochs, selected by the environment variable `FORK_HORIZONS_CACHE_ONLY=1` (read where the
default fetcher is chosen); a test that a miss raises and a hit does not touch the network. The
M10 launch command sets it. No cached state changes. This is the "cache-only fetcher for
pre-registered runs" item of the v1.1 list.

## Amendment (2026-11-10, on approval; before any M10 code)

The plan above is approved with three amendments. Nothing above this line is edited.

1. **Tight-posterior threshold.** If the rebinned 95 % width of S3's posterior marginal in a
   lies within ±0.2 AU of 8 AU (7.8–8.2 AU), the posterior's reading is **borderline**: no
   tight/loose claim is made, and the profile × posterior table's third row ("flat, tight") is
   not applied. The width is reported to two decimals either way.
2. **Leave-one-out profiles.** All four leave-one-out profiles are reported in every case, as
   description (the curves, the per-point tables and the comparison rows). The interpretation
   rows (iii) and (iv) are read only if A is constrained under Student-t, as planned.
3. **The scale of the reading rule.** Q is on the χ² scale (−2 ln L up to a constant). The
   reading rule, the crossings and every Δ ln L in the tables use Δ ln L = −ΔQ/2, where
   ΔQ = Q_min(a) − min over the grid of Q_min, against the thresholds −0.5 and −2 (ΔQ = 1
   and 4). The same holds for S3's marginal where it is read beside Q.

### Interpretation

- **No row applies.** A is partial under Student-t, so neither (i) nor (ii) is read, and
  (iii)/(iv) are not read either. By the rule: the curves and the failed clauses are reported
  and no conclusion is drawn beyond them. The A × B table is not used for the Student-t pair.
- **Description, not a rule reading.** Under Student-t the upper side of A's profile is far
  flatter than under Gaussian errors: the −2 interval reaches 40.55 AU instead of 33.99, and Le
  Verrier's 36.15 AU moves from −3.15 to −0.76. The lower side keeps its shape under both
  models (28 AU at −3.49 against −1.85; 26 AU at −16.6 against −18.0). A − Flamsteed is flat
  and changes solution family (longitude 313°, mass 23–32 M⊕, Uranus flagged everywhere);
  removing Bradley, Mayer or Le Monnier leaves A's curve almost unchanged. Had A been
  constrained, (iii) would have named Flamsteed; under the rule it is description.

## Pre-registration (written 2026-11-09, before any M9 code or run)

Or is there a ridge along which a trades against the mass and the orbit's shape, so that a
comes from the prior and the cell structure rather than from the data? The same question is
asked without the 19 observations before Herschel's discovery in 1781. Those are the rows that
give the fit its 155-year lever arm.

### What is maximised

At each grid point, a is held and the profile is taken over everything else.
- **The perturber:** log₁₀ m, √e cos ϖ, √e sin ϖ, λ₀ and the two inclination parameters.
- **Uranus's in-plane (a, e cos ϖ, e sin ϖ, λ₀):** docs/RULES.md rule 2, always free. Its i
  and Ω are held.
- **The eight group offsets.**

The perturber is bounded to the headline's prior support: 1–100 M⊕, e ≤ 0.3, i ≤ 10°
relative to Uranus's plane. The profile then reads the same model the headline samples.
- Any bound that is active at an optimum is reported.
- Uranus and the offsets are unbounded. Uranus's distance from the centre of its prior box is
  reported, in box half-widths.

**The profiled quantity** is ln L(a) = −½ χ²_min(a).
- χ² is the headline's augmented χ²: the whitened residuals plus, for each of the 8 group
  offsets, its prior term (b_g/τ_g)², as in `RaDecObservations.whitened`.
- χ² is minimised over everything except a: the perturber's mass, e, angles and inclination,
  Uranus's four in-plane elements, and the 8 offsets (in closed form).
- The alternative would integrate the offsets out, as the headline's sampler does. The two give
  the same Δ ln L(a):
  - the offsets enter linearly with a Gaussian prior, and their precision matrix (per group,
    N_g/σ_g² + 1/τ_g²) depends on no parameter;
  - so the integral differs from the minimum by the same ln det at every a, which cancels in
    Δ ln L.
- Uranus's elements are maximised, not integrated out. That is what makes ln L(a) a profile
  likelihood.

**Search: the M8 perturbative likelihood.** `fork.model.perturbative.PerturbativeProblem`, as
in the M8 runs: `second_order=False`, Δs = 60 d, cubic quadrature, u_ref from `M8_u_ref.json`.
- In it, Uranus's four coordinates are solved by the linear step about u_ref, and the offsets
  in closed form. Its χ² (`GaussianMarginal.chi2`) is therefore already minimised over both.
  J_u is fixed at u_ref, so the ln det term is constant in the perturber's parameters.
- Dataset B has no posterior of its own, so it uses the headline's u_ref. A B solution whose
  Uranus lies more than 3 posterior standard deviations from u_ref (`posterior_std_u` of the
  headline) is flagged. The exact step below does not depend on u_ref.
- The optimiser is `scipy.optimize.least_squares` (trust-region reflective) on the whitened
  vector, in the sampler's q coordinates. The e ≤ 0.3 disk is enforced by the existing guard
  in `fork.inference.fit` (`_fit_bounded`).

**Starts per grid point and dataset: 40, then continuation.**
1. **32 draws from the headline prior at the held a.** The draws use
   `np.random.default_rng([2026, 9, d, k])`, where d = 0 for A and 1 for B, and k = 0–14 is the
   index of the grid or reference point.
   - log₁₀ m uniform on [0, 2].
   - (√e cos ϖ, √e sin ϖ) uniform on the disk e ≤ 0.3.
   - λ₀ uniform on [0, 2π).
   - cos i uniform on [cos 10°, 1] and Ω uniform.
2. **8 starts from the M6c replacement posterior** (the headline's, for both datasets): from
   the 200 samples nearest the grid point in ln a, the 8 with the highest likelihood, with a
   set to the grid value.
3. **Continuation:** each grid point is restarted from its neighbours' best solutions, until no
   point improves by more than 0.01 in χ². Then one sweep left to right and one right to left
   carry any lower branch across the grid. These are the conventions of
   `fork.inference.profile`.
   - The two reference points join the continuation as ordinary points, in order of a, but the
     reading rule ignores them.

The random draws and the continuation are there because the M5a profile once missed a branch
that no fixed start reached (M5.md, the a ≈ 22 AU, e = 0.3 branch).

**Confirmation: the exact likelihood.** The full model is `fork.model.forward` with the
project's Wisdom–Holman integrator, exact apparent places and the same augmented χ².
- At each grid point, the best perturbative optimum is polished with the exact likelihood,
  over all ten parameters at the held a. So is any other distinct optimum within 4 of the best
  in perturbative χ² (at most 3 in all).
  - Distinct means the perturber's geocentric longitude on 23 September 1846 differs by
    more than 2°, or its e by more than 0.03.
- The lowest exact χ² is the profile value.
- The perturbative χ² is reported beside it for every optimum.

The reading uses the exact values. M8's A8a validated the perturbative likelihood only within
Δ ln L ≤ 30 of the posterior, with errors of up to 1.5 in χ² outside the core. The grid's ends
(24, 40, 44 AU) may lie well outside that.

**Checks before reading.**
- Every exact polish ends on scipy's convergence criterion (status > 0).
- The continuation has converged.

If a grid point fails, it is rerun once with 40 more random starts (rng seed
`[2026, 9, d, k, 1]`), and the failure is reported. A point that still fails is reported as
missing and is not used by the reading rule.

### Reported, per dataset and grid point

- Δ ln L(a) = ln L(a) − max over the grid of ln L (exact; per dataset).
- The fitted mass (M⊕), e and inclination.
- The planet's geocentric longitude on Galle's night, 23 September 1846 (JD TDB from
  `m6c_conjunction.json`, light-time corrected, ecliptic of date, as the headline).
- Any active bound; Uranus's box distance; perturbative minus exact χ².
- **The maximum, per dataset:** a_max is the grid point with the highest exact ln L, with its
  mass. A parabola through a_max and its two neighbours gives an interpolated maximum. The
  parabola is reported only; the rule uses grid points.
- **At the two reference points, per dataset:** Δ ln L at a = 30.07 AU (Neptune) and at
  a = 36.15 AU (Le Verrier), each with the best-fit mass there and its Galle's-night longitude.
  Δ ln L is relative to the same maximum as the grid, or to the reference point if that is
  higher (reported if so).

### Profile against the headline posterior's marginal in a

The headline posterior exists for dataset A only, so this comparison is made for A. B's
profile is shown beside it, without a posterior.
- **The marginal:** the M6c replacement posterior's density in a, from its weighted samples
  (`PartitionedResult`). It is drawn as ln p(a) − max ln p on the same axis as Δ ln L(a), with
  its 68 % and 95 % intervals marked.
- **The profile's intervals:** where Δ ln L = −0.5 and −2, by linear interpolation between grid
  points. These are reported beside the posterior's 68 % and 95 % intervals.
- **The volume term:** at each grid point, ln p(a) − ln L(a) (both relative to their maxima).
  The posterior is the likelihood integrated over the other parameters, times the prior. So
  where the profile is flat but the marginal falls, this term shows how much of the fall comes
  from the prior and the volume of the other parameters (mass, e, angles) at that a.
  - The density is a histogram of the samples in ln a with bins one grid step wide, and the
    term is reported only where a bin holds at least 50 effective samples.
- **Tight** means the posterior's 95 % interval in a is narrower than 2X = 8 AU. It is 4.1 AU
  now (28.65–32.75).

|---|---|---|

| constrained | 95 % interval more than twice as wide as the profile's | The volume of the other parameters broadens the posterior beyond what the data fix at the best fit. The report gives the volume term. |

### Reading rule (fixed now)

**Two thresholds:**
- **Δ ln L = −2.** For one parameter this is Δχ² = 4, about 2σ.
- **X = 4 AU**, justified below.

**Each dataset's profile reads as exactly one of three:**
1. **Constrained:**
   - a_max is not at an end of the grid (24 or 44 AU);
   - on each side of a_max, some grid point within a_max ± 4 AU has Δ ln L < −2;
   - and every grid point farther than 4 AU from a_max has Δ ln L < −2 (no second branch).
2. **Flat:** Δ ln L > −2 at every grid point from 26 to 44 AU.
3. **Partial:** neither. Examples: it falls below −2 only farther than 4 AU out; a second
   branch rises above −2; or a_max is at the end of the grid. The report says which.

**Why X = 4 AU:**
- **Expected scale.** If the profile matched the headline posterior, Δ ln L would reach −2
  near the posterior's 95 % limits: 28.65 and 32.75 AU, which are 1.6 and 2.5 AU from its
  median of 30.22. X = 4 AU leaves room on the wide side, for two reasons.
  - A profile can be wider than a marginal along a ridge. The e ≈ 0.06–0.12 ridge of M6c's
    R2 runs reaches a ≈ 32.5 AU at its 84th percentile (`M6c_r2_ridge.json`).
  - The grid is spaced 2 AU beyond 32 AU.
- **Distinguishing Le Verrier's distance.** X is under the 6.1 AU between Neptune's a (30.07
  AU) and Le Verrier's (36.15 AU). Together with the no-second-branch clause, "constrained"
  with a_max near 30 AU means the data put Le Verrier's a below −2.
- **On the grid.** With a_max = 30 AU, the window's ends, 26 and 34 AU, are grid points.

**Interpretation (A × B).** The cases below are the four that the rule reads cleanly.

| A (all rows) | B (no pre-1781 rows) | reading |
|---|---|---|
| constrained | constrained | The modern series alone fix the distance. The ancient sightings are not what pins a, and Le Verrier's 36 AU is excluded by the data either way. |
| constrained | flat | The distance comes from the pre-1781 sightings: the 155-year lever arm. Without them the data allow any a from 26 to 44 AU at Δ ln L > −2. The headline's a then rests on 37 measurements by four observers, whose reduction (τ_ancient, M6.md) and transcription matter most. |
| flat | flat | The data do not fix the distance. The headline's a interval then comes from the prior and the posterior's volume (mass, e and angles), not from a likelihood peak, and the report says so. The longitude on Galle's night at each a then shows whether the prediction survives along the ridge. |
| flat | constrained | Removing data sharpened the profile: the ancient rows pull against the modern ones. No physical reading is offered. The report gives the ancient group's χ² share along the grid and lists this as a tension to examine. |

- **When either profile is partial:** the table is not used for it. The report gives both
  curves, says which clause failed and where, and draws no conclusion beyond that.
- **Whatever the outcome:** M9 changes no published number. Whether its reading goes into
  the README or the site is the user's decision afterwards.

### The reading rule

- **A: constrained.** a_max = 30 AU is interior; on the left 26 AU (−17.96) and 28 AU (−1.85, not
  below −2, so the clause is met by 26 AU) and on the right 34 AU (−2.01) lie within 4 AU with
  Δ ln L < −2; every point farther than 4 AU (24, 36, 40, 44 AU) is below −2. No second branch.
- **B: flat.** Every point from 26 to 44 AU is above −2 (the lowest is −1.09 at 26 AU); the
  constrained clauses fail (nothing within 4 AU falls below −2, and 24, 36, 40 and 44 AU are
  above −2).
- **A × B, constrained and flat:** "The distance comes from the pre-1781 sightings: the
  155-year lever arm. Without them the data allow any a from 26 to 44 AU at Δ ln L > −2. The
  headline's a then rests on 37 measurements by four observers, whose reduction (τ_ancient,
  M6.md) and transcription matter most."
- A's ancient-group χ² along the grid (table above): 40.5–44.1 across 28–40 AU, 53.4 at 24 and
  59.8 at 44 AU: the pre-1781 rows are what the far ends of the grid cannot fit.

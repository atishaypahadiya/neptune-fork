# M2: Kepler solver

## Built
`fork/integrators/kepler.py`, all `numba.njit`:
- `stumpff_c2_c3(ψ)`: a 13-term series for |ψ| < 1 (truncation ≪ ε). The closed form elsewhere
  uses the half-angle form for c₂ (2 sin²(√ψ/2)/ψ), so 1 − cos never cancels. Includes a
  hyperbolic branch.
- `kepler_fg_scalar` / `kepler_step` / `kepler_drift_inplace`: a universal-variable f–g
  propagator.
  - **Elliptic Δt is reduced modulo the period**, so a long Δt costs the same as a short one.
  - After that reduction χ lies in a known bracket |χ| ≤ 2π/√α, because |ΔM| ≤ π ⇒
    |ΔE| < π + 2e < 2π.
  - Newton from a second-order guess, safeguarded by the bracket. A step that leaves it becomes
    bisection, so convergence is guaranteed. f(χ) is monotone, since f′ = r > 0.
  - Hyperbolic orbits get a bracket by doubling outward.
  - The new state is formed as r₀ + (f−1)r₀ + g v₀ so the many short WH steps don't lose digits.

## Missed target: full period < 1e-12 AU for e ≳ 0.6 at large a
**Cause: double-precision conditioning, not the solver.** At pericenter
1/a = 2/r − v²/μ cancels by about (1+e)/(1−e), which is ≈ 40× at e = 0.95. So the energy (and the
period) of any rounded state is uncertain at ~40 ε ≈ 4e-15. That timing error, multiplied by
v_peri·P ≈ 39 a, gives ≈ 5e-12 AU at a = 19, the observed order of magnitude. Evidence that the
solver isn't at fault:
- Against an analytic ellipse built from the *same rounded state*, the error is 1.2e-13·a even
  at e = 0.95.
- Error scales smoothly with (1+e)/(1−e) and with a, and is ~40 ulp even for circular orbits.

**What the tests assert:** < 1e-12 AU for e ≤ 0.3, which covers every planet (Uranus e = 0.047)
and the M5 perturber prior (e ≤ 0.3). For e ∈ {0.6, 0.8, 0.95} they assert the measured-scale
bound 1e-11·a, with the reasoning in the docstring. Not loosened silently: flagged here and in the
report.

## Surprises
- The first full-period test used P from the *input* a. The rounded state implies a slightly
  different period, and that alone causes ~1e-14 relative error, even for circular orbits. Tests
  now use the state's own α everywhere.
- **Bug found and fixed while checking a claim in this note.** A draft of this note said "the
  bisection fallback never triggered". Instrumenting it (`kepler_fg_diag` returns iteration and
  bisection counts) showed **12,782 bisection steps in 60,000 calls**, including tiny steps.
  - Cause: the convergence test ran *after* the bracket test. At convergence, lo or hi equals χ,
    so the final sub-ulp Newton step looked like it was leaving the bracket and triggered a
    bisection jump to the middle of a possibly huge bracket. Results were still correct, just
    slow.
  - Fixed by testing convergence first. A better guess for long steps (χ = √μ α Δt when
    |Δt| > 5% P) brought the total down to 611, and to zero on the WH hot path. A regression test
    pins this.

# M8: Fast linearized likelihood

Spec: `docs/PHASE1_SPEC.md`, "M8: Fast linearized likelihood" (b65a7f0). M8 lives in new
modules. The M6c pipeline (`fork.export.m6c`, `fork.export.m6c_repeat`) and its frozen inputs
are not changed.

## Implementation plan (written before any M8 code, 2026-11-08)

This plan covers two stages:
- **Stage 1:** the module and its unit tests.
- **Stage 2:** A8a, and A8e as a per-call timing. Stage 2 runs no nested sampling.

A8b–A8f involve sampling. They get their own pre-registration commit later.

The new files are:
- `src/fork/model/linearized.py`: the likelihood.
- `src/fork/export/m8.py`: the A8a and A8e driver, which writes `site/data/m8_validation.json`.
- `tests/test_linearized.py`.

### What is marginalized

- **u** is `q[:4]`, Uranus's (a, e cos ϖ, e sin ϖ, λ₀) in ecl_j2000. These are the four
  coordinates of the M6c prior box (`UranusBox`). Uranus's i and Ω stay held (docs/RULES.md
  rule 2).
- **φ** is `q[4:]`, the perturber's 5 parameters plus its 2 inclination parameters.
- **The group offsets** b_g are already marginalized exactly inside
  `RaDecObservations.whitened`, for any u.
- **z(u, φ)** is that augmented whitened vector. ‖z‖² is the offset-marginal −2 ln L, up to a
  constant that depends on neither u nor φ.

Integrating exp(−½‖z‖²) over u after the offsets equals the spec's joint (u, b) marginal. The
reason: JᵀJ of the offset-profiled vector is the Schur complement of the joint precision, that
is, the u block after b is integrated out. For a residual linear in u:

    −2 ln L(φ) = χ²_min(φ) + ln det(JᵀJ) − 4 ln 2π + 2 ln V_box

- V_box is the box volume in q units, and the prior density on u is 1/V_box.
- So ∫ L(φ) π(φ) dφ is the same Z that the M6c runs estimate, with the same Gaussian constant
  dropped. M8's ln Z_k are therefore directly comparable with M6c's, as A8b and A8c need.

### Jacobian: finite differences, not variational equations

- **Mode (a)** needs J once, at the Uranus-only fit, so its cost does not matter. It uses
  central differences.
- **Mode (b)** needs a fresh J for every φ. I chose finite differences over variational
  equations:
  - Variational equations would mean differentiating the Wisdom–Holman map: the Kepler drift
    (f and g functions with the universal-variable solver), the interaction kick, and the
    democratic-heliocentric transforms.
  - They would also mean differentiating the apparent-place chain: light time, aberration,
    and precession–nutation.
  - That is new numba code inside the integrator, and it would itself have to be validated
    against finite differences.
  - Forward differences cost 4 extra integrations, 6 per call in all, which the spec allows.
- **If mode (a) passes A8a, J per call is never needed.** Variational equations are worth
  building only if mode (b) is chosen and its 6 integrations per call make A8e fail.
- **Steps:** 0.01 raw σ per coordinate (raw σ = box σ / √(χ²/dof)). The algebra works in
  coordinates scaled by raw σ, because a, k, h and λ₀ differ by four orders of magnitude.
- **Testing a finite-difference J.** Because J is itself a finite difference, "J against
  finite differences" compares independent schemes:
  - central differences at steps h and 2h, Richardson-extrapolated;
  - the forward differences that mode (b) uses.

  The test also checks the linear prediction z(u + δ) − z(u) ≈ Jδ along random directions.

### Reference trajectory per perturber sample

- **Mode (a)** linearizes about u_ref = the box centre, which is the Uranus-only fit to the
  data alone, for every φ.
  - J₀ = ∂z/∂u is computed there without the perturber, as the spec says.
  - Each call is one integration, z₀ = z(u_ref, φ), followed by one Gauss–Newton step to get
    u*(φ) and χ²_min = ‖z₀ + J₀Δu‖².
  - The ln det term is the same constant for every φ.
- **Mode (b)** starts as mode (a), then refines:
  - it integrates at u₁ = u*(a);
  - it evaluates J₁ at u₁ by forward differences;
  - it takes a second Gauss–Newton step;
  - χ²_min and ln det come from (z₁, J₁).

  That is 6 integrations per call.
- **The spec chooses between them:** mode (a) if it passes A8a, otherwise (b), otherwise
  stop.

**Risk for mode (a).** u* sits far from u_ref:
- The R2 posterior median is at (−37.6, +41.2, +4.6, −17.4) box-σ from the centre.
- One box-σ is 4.0 raw σ here (the Uranus-only fit has χ²/dof = 16.0), so that is up to
  about 165 raw σ.
- Mode (a)'s single linearization spans that whole distance. A rough second-order estimate
  for k gives about 0.06″ per measurement, which could exceed |Δχ²| < 0.1.
- Mode (b) re-linearizes at u₁, which is within a few raw σ of u*.

### Issue 1: the prior on u is a box, not flat

Analytic marginalization integrates over all of R⁴, but the real prior is uniform on the
±50 box-σ box. The formula is exact only if N(u*, (JᵀJ)⁻¹) puts negligible mass outside the
box.

**Why the truncation is negligible.** The box-edge checks in M6.md (2026-11-07, all
variants) put every posterior median at least 6.4 posterior std from the nearest edge: 12.4
for the headline (k) and 6.4 for S2 (a). No sample comes within 5.4 box-σ of an edge.
- At 12 std a Gaussian's outside mass is below 1e-33.
- The conditional covariance (JᵀJ)⁻¹ is narrower than the marginal posterior std, which also
  carries the spread in φ. So the distance that matters here is larger still.

**Measured, not assumed.**
- `truncation_bound` returns a rigorous upper bound on the mass outside the box. It is a
  union bound over the 8 faces using each coordinate's conditional σ, computed from log
  survival functions so that tiny values do not underflow.
- The function also returns −2 ln(1 − bound), the matching bound on the error in −2 ln L.
- A test checks the bound against brute-force integration on a toy problem with a narrow box.
- A8a reports the bound at every point.

**Spec conflict, flagged.** The spec says the largest |u*(φ) − centre| must stay below
40 box-σ, or the truncation is not negligible.
- The full-likelihood posteriors already break this rule: R2 samples reach |k − centre| =
  44.4 box-σ (median 41.2), and the headline's reach 44.2.
- The rule measures distance from the centre, but what matters is the distance to the edge in
  posterior std.
- I will report the number as the spec asks and will not change the rule. I propose to
  replace it with the measured truncation bound in the A8b–A8f pre-registration; that is the
  user's decision.

### Issue 2: S3 (Student-t) has no analytic marginal

With t errors, −2 ln L is not quadratic in the residuals. Even with residuals linear in u, the
integrand over u is not Gaussian. The offset marginal is already done by quadrature
(`fork.model.student_t`).

Options considered:
1. **Gaussian scale mixture.** Give each measurement a latent precision
   w_i ~ Gamma(ν/2, ν/2). Given the w's, (u, b) marginalize analytically, but then the 529
   w's have to be sampled or integrated, which moves the problem into 529 dimensions.
   Rejected.
2. **Laplace in u only.** Find u*(φ) of the t marginal by Gauss–Newton with iteratively
   reweighted least squares (the t weights settle within a few iterations). Then
   −2 ln L ≈ −2 ln L_t(u*) + ln det(H/2) + const, with H the Hessian of −2 ln L_t in u.
   - It is not analytic: each reweighting iteration costs one integration.
   - Its error has to be measured against importance-sampling integration over u at a set of
     S3 posterior points.
3. **Out of scope.**

**Proposal.** A8d's S3 part is out of scope for analytic M8. Then one of two things:
- option 2, with its own acceptance test against importance sampling, pre-registered with
  A8b–A8f; or
- S3 is checked another way: a full-likelihood repeat of S3's cell 2 at R2's settings,
  2 seeds, as A8d's fail rule already provides.

Stage 2 does not use S3. `LinearizedProblem` refuses Student-t observations.

### A8a in Stage 2

The spec asks for 2000 draws each from R2 and from S1–S3. Stage 2 uses fewer, per this
stage's instruction (≥ 500 points: R2, S1 and prior draws):

| source | points | selection |
|---|---|---|
| R2 posterior | 600 | stratified: 300 core (e < 0.06), 300 ridge (e ≥ 0.06) |
| S1 posterior | 300 | as drawn |
| headline prior | 150 | the full prior |
| headline prior, ridge-branch region | 150 | a 28–33 AU, e 0.06–0.12 |

S2 and S3 draws are not in this stage.

At each point:
- mode (a) and mode (b);
- the full profile χ²_min: Levenberg–Marquardt over u, unconstrained, started at mode (b)'s
  u*;
- the Laplace −2 ln L with the exact Hessian: the Gauss–Newton term plus Σ z_k ∇²z_k, with
  second derivatives by central differences at 0.5 raw σ.

Thresholds (spec): |Δχ²| < 0.1 and |Δ(−2 ln L)| < 0.2.
- They apply to points within Δ ln L = 30 of the best.
- Prior-tail points are reported, but they lie outside the spec's test domain.
- Results are given per class (core, ridge, other cells, prior tail) and per mode as
  distributions: quantiles and maximum, not only the maximum.
- Each point also records |u* − centre| in box-σ and the truncation bound.

### A8e in Stage 2

Per-call time, single thread, for the full χ² (the M6c path), mode (a) and mode (b).

A8e's pass condition is per fit: at most 1/10 of 28.5 CPU-h. Mode (a) costs one integration
per call, the same as the full likelihood. So any speed-up has to come from fewer calls:
7 sampled dimensions instead of 11, and no 18-nat compression in Uranus's coordinates. That
can only be measured by sampling, in Stage 3.

Long runs log progress to `data/cache/m8/progress.log` and resume from chunk files there.

### After-the-fact diagnostic, not a spec mode: (b′), mode (b) with J₀ reused

Because mode (a)'s error is all in the residual and none in J, I measured a variant on the
same 1200 points, labelled here and not adopted. It is mode (b)'s second integration at u₁,
with the second Gauss–Newton step taken with J₀ instead of a forward-difference J₁. That is 2
integrations per call instead of 6. Script: `docs/notes/scripts/m8_fixed_j_refinement.py`.
Output: `M8_fixed_j_refinement.json`.

- **Posterior points (900):** largest |Δχ²| 2.3e-4 and largest |Δ(−2 ln L)| 2.0e-3, about
  400 and 100 times inside the tolerances. None is over.
- **Prior tail:** 30 points over tolerance, the closest at Δ ln L = 15,072.
- **Cost:** 10.99 ms per call, 1.99 × the full likelihood. A8e would then need about 20 ×
  fewer calls instead of 60 ×.
- **Compared with variational equations:** they would cut mode (b) to about 2 integrations
  per call. (b′) gets the same cost without them, because J changes negligibly between the
  box centre and u*.

### Decisions for the A8b–A8f pre-registration (the user's)

1. **The mode.** Either (b), as the spec's rule chose it, with A8e likely to fail, or (b′),
   pre-registered as a third mode with this A8a evidence.
2. **The 40 box-σ rule.** Replace it with the measured truncation bound (already broken by the
   full-likelihood posterior at 43.8).
3. **S3.** Out of scope for analytic M8; checked by a full-likelihood repeat, or a Laplace-in-u
   extension with its own test (plan, issue 2).
4. **Tail guard for sampling.** Where u* lies outside the box, the R⁴ marginal overstates the
   likelihood. All such points seen are at Δ ln L ≥ 15,000, but the sampler should add the
   truncation term or flag those points, rather than rely on that.

## Decisions after Stage 2 (2026-11-08, the user's)

1. **Neither (b) nor (b′) is adopted.** Linearizing Uranus is accurate: mode (b) passes A8a
   with errors below 1e-5 in χ² and 2e-5 in −2 ln L. But no mode of this kind can pass A8e,
   because every call still integrates the perturber.
   - The per-call integration is the bottleneck. Measured on the M6c path, a call is 5.42 ms of
     integration and 0.053 ms of everything else (apparent places, residuals, the offset
     marginal).
   - Removing Uranus from the sampler cannot make a call cheaper than one integration. Mode (b)
     costs 6 integrations, (b′) costs 2, and variational equations would also cost 2.
   - The validated part, marginalizing Uranus's four coordinates and the offsets analytically,
     carries over to the amendment below.
2. **The 40 box-σ rule is replaced** by the measured truncation bound
   (`fork.model.linearized.truncation_bound`). The spec text is amended to match.
3. **S3 is out of scope for analytic M8.** The spec text is amended to match.
4. **Solutions outside the box are flagged, with no truncation term.** A call whose u* lies
   outside the box returns a flag beside its likelihood. A run reports how many flagged points
   it met and the highest likelihood among them.

The spec now carries an amendment drafting a first-order perturbative likelihood (PHASE1_SPEC,
"M8 amendment: first-order perturbative likelihood"). It is spec text only: no code, and not
yet pre-registered.

**2026-11-08, later:**
- The 10⁻⁶ truncation threshold in −2 ln L is approved.
- The perturbative amendment now has three more parts:
  - An error-propagation estimate. A coherent 0.1″ error over 529 measurements at σ ≈ 2″
    gives |Δχ²| of about 1–4, so pointwise A8a is at risk.
  - A fast-then-reweight pipeline. The published results are the reweighted ones, with ln Z
    corrected by the mean weight.
  - A new gate: reweighting ESS/N ≥ 0.5 overall and ≥ 0.3 within each ridge branch. A8a
    becomes a diagnostic, and A8b–A8d and A8f apply to the reweighted results with their
    tolerances unchanged.
- The amendment also states the reweighting blind spot and the four checks that cover it.
- It is spec text only, not yet pre-registered.

## Perturbative likelihood: build, A8a error budget, A8e per call (2026-11-09)

Per the amended spec (5fc089f). New modules only; M6c's pipeline, Stage 2's `m8.py` and the
frozen inputs are unchanged. No sampling yet, so the reweighting gate itself is not measured.

**Built**
- `fork.model.perturbative` (the likelihood):
  - The reference is Uranus with the Sun, Jupiter and Saturn, from the median u of each data
    set's M6c posterior (the replacement posterior for the headline).
  - Φ comes from central finite differences (12 integrations). Its scaled symplectic deviation
    is 4.4 × 10⁻⁸.
  - The grid is a 60-day step plus every observation epoch: 1223 nodes.
  - The quadrature is a cubic per interval, with weights precomputed from the grid.
  - The perturber follows a Kepler orbit about the Sun–Jupiter–Saturn barycentre. Its direct
    and indirect pull is computed at all nodes, and the mass factors out.
  - Apparent places are exact. Uranus and the offsets are marginalized as in Stage 1.
  - The second-order switch is one Picard step: the perturber's pull is re-evaluated along the
    first-order path, plus the nonlinear remainder of the Sun–Jupiter–Saturn field. The
    remainder is as large as the change in the pull, so it can't be dropped.
  - Precomputation takes 0.12 CPU-s.
- `fork.inference.reweight`:
  - per-cell weights and the ln Z correction, with its Monte Carlo error √((N/ESS − 1)/N);
  - cells combined like `PartitionedResult`, with only the sampler's part of the ln Z error
    inflated;
  - ESS overall and within any subset of samples.
- `fork.export.m8_perturbative` runs A8a, A8e and the summary, writing
  `site/data/m8_perturbative.json`.
- Tests (`tests/test_perturbative.py`, `tests/test_reweight.py`, all fast):
  - Φ against direct propagation: the error is the orbit's own nonlinearity, halving with the
    amplitude.
  - Kepler propagation against closed-form elements.
  - The toy perturber against the full integrator.
  - The second-order switch.
  - Quadrature convergence.
  - With no forcing, the result is Stage 1's marginal.
  - Reweighting on toy Gaussians, against the closed-form ESS and ln Z correction.

**A term the spec's budget left out: E_JS.** In the full model, Jupiter and Saturn respond to
the perturber (directly and through the Sun's reflex), and that changes their pull on Uranus.
This is first order in the mass, so it does not shrink with it.
- At Neptune's mass Saturn moves 0.0013 AU.
- On a 0.01 M⊕ toy, with the perturber's exact trajectory, the left-out forcing is the whole
  residual: 4.9 × 10⁻⁵ relative without it, 1.4 × 10⁻⁶ with it.
- It is measured below as its own term. I did not change the model to include it.

  - Core: 162 of 786 points have |Δχ²| ≥ 0.1, and 10 have |Δ(−2 ln L)| ≥ 0.2, with the switch
    on.
  - The largest in-domain error is 0.30 in the core and ridges, and 1.5 in the other cells.
  - The signed medians are 0.003–0.02, so most of the error is scatter, not offset.

- **The quadrature, the reference distance and the box truncation are negligible.** Truncation
  bounds are ≤ 10⁻¹³⁸ on in-domain points, and no in-domain point lies outside the box.
- **Prior tail (300 points, none in the domain):**

  - The fast likelihood is mostly too high there (206 of 300 by more than 3 in ln L). That is

  - The 26 points where it is too low by more than 3 lie at Δ ln L ≥ 4680 from the best.
  - **Blind-spot screen (spec check 1):** no in-domain point has the fast ln L too low by more
    than 3, switch off or on.

- **E_JS:** the left-out first-order term above.

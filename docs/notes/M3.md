# M3: Integrators (A1)

## Built
All `numba.njit` with on-disk cache. Signature `integrate(x0, v0, masses, t_out, dt)` returns
heliocentric positions `[t_out, body, 3]`; `integrate_states` also returns velocities.
- **Inputs:** x0 and v0 are heliocentric (N, 3). masses is (N+1,) with the central body first.
- **Output times:** t_out is relative to the epoch, in any order, and may be negative (reached by
  integrating backward). Off-grid outputs use a partial step on a *copy* of the state, so
  requesting outputs never changes the trajectory (tested bit-for-bit).
- `rk4.py`: classical RK4 in barycentric coordinates, all-pairs gravity. The non-symplectic
  baseline.
- `leapfrog.py`: KDK Störmer–Verlet, barycentric.
- `wisdom_holman.py`: democratic heliocentric (DLL98): Q = heliocentric positions,
  V = barycentric velocities, μ = G·m₀. Two compositions:
  - `SCHEME_KICK_OUTER` (**default, as in the spec**): kick/2 → jump/2 → Kepler → jump/2 →
    kick/2. One Kepler solve per step.
  - `SCHEME_DRIFT_OUTER` (**REBOUND WHFast-DH's composition**, read from REBOUND 5.2.2
    `integrator_whfast.c`): Kepler/2 → jump/2 → kick → jump/2 → Kepler/2.
- `_nbody.py`: output planning, helio ↔ bary conversion, all-pairs accelerations, total energy.
- `tests/data/horizons_a1_1781-03-13.json`: the Horizons states at 1781-03-13 TDB, committed
  (public-domain JPL data, 24 numbers), so A1 runs in CI without network. A `network` test checks
  it against live Horizons.
- `docs/notes/M3_energy.png` (script: `docs/notes/scripts/m3_energy_plot.py`): the RK4 vs
  symplectic energy panel for M7.

## Two A1 targets missed, and why
Both misses are now `xfail(strict=True)` tests carrying the measured numbers, so they stay visible
and will flag if they ever start passing. Neither is loosened.

1. **0.049″ vs REBOUND (spec order).** This isn't an implementation error.
   - The REBOUND-order path matches REBOUND to 4.8e-9″, which verifies every piece: Kepler solver,
     kick, jump, coordinate transforms, masses, μ.
   - The spec order converges to IAS15 at exactly O(dt²): ×4 per halving, 0.033″ → 0.00051″.
   - The two compositions are different second-order maps. Their dt = 20 d errors are 0.033″
     and 0.016″ with opposite signs, so they differ by 0.049″.
   - "Same ICs, same dt" against WHFast is only a < 0.01″ test if the composition is also the
     same.
2. **Energy 2–4e-8 > 1e-8.** REBOUND's own WHFast-DH gives 2.00e-8 at dt = 20 d, the same as our
   REBOUND-order run. This is the standard O(ε (n_J dt)²) oscillating WH error, with ~217 steps
   per Jupiter orbit. It's bounded: the last century's max equals the first's, and the trend is
   1e-10/kyr. Jacobi coordinates don't help energy (REBOUND: 2.2e-8).

Scientific impact: none at our noise level. The worst truncation error (0.033″ over 65 yr) is
1% of the 3″ observational σ and 0.03% of the ~130″ signal.

**Options (user's call):**
- (a) switch the default to `SCHEME_DRIFT_OUTER` and amend the spec's ordering. That gives an
  exact REBOUND match, and it's 2× more accurate here.
- (b) keep the spec order; amend A1 to compare the like-for-like composition, and make the energy
  target "bounded, no drift, < 5e-8".
- (c) keep everything and set default dt = 8 d (2.5× cost, still ~1.6 ms per run).

## Other notes
- **RK4 is far more accurate in position than WH at the same dt** (5e-5″ vs 0.03″). It loses on
  long-term energy behaviour and costs 4 force evaluations per step. For M4–M5 (65–160 yr spans),
  WH's advantage is speed and robustness, not accuracy.
- Leapfrog's KDK error, 3.4e-5 at dt = 20 d, is bounded but O((n dt)²) in the *full* Kepler
  potential. It's the textbook illustration of why WH splits Kepler out.
- Gotcha: REBOUND 5 dropped `sim.ri_whfast`. Use `sim.integrator.coordinates = "..."`.
- numba can't on-disk-cache a function that takes another jitted function as an argument. So each
  integrator has its own small driver loop around a shared `plan_outputs`.

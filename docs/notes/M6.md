# M6: Historical data

## M6a stop condition: what Le Verrier's memoir tabulates

Source read: Le Verrier, *Recherches sur les mouvements de la planète Herschel (dite Uranus)*,
Paris, Bachelier, 1846. This is the offprint of *Connaissance des Temps pour 1849*, Additions,
pp. 3–254. Scan used: archive.org `bub_gb_HMi0NyDVkhQC`, a Google Books copy with 264 PDF
pages, page images 1608 × 2500 px:
https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_HMi0NyDVkhQC. Printed page *p* is PDF page *p* + 6 (index
*p* + 5) in the table pages checked.

**Answer: the memoir tabulates observed positions, one row per observation, with Le Verrier's
O − C beside them.** So positions can be recovered without his tables, and the source plan
stands. There are three levels.

### 1. Table n° 77, pp. 129–136: individual observations

"Tableau de la comparaison des observations d'Uranus avec la théorie admise." The 11 columns,
as printed (described in the text, p. 127):

| # | column | notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Années | year |
| 2 | Mois et jours | civil date |
| 3 | Temps moyen | mean time of the observation, h m s. The meridian is not stated in the table (see open questions) |
| 4 | Ascension droite observée | modern rows in time (h m s, to 0.01 s); ancient rows in arc (° ′ ″, to 0.1″) |
| 5 | Secondes de l'AR calculée | seconds only, from the geocentric ephemeris n° 75 |
| 6 | AR calculée moins AR observée | C − O, s (modern) or ″ (ancient) |
| 7 | Déclinaison observée | ° ′ ″, to 0.1″ |
| 8 | Secondes de la déclinaison calculée | from n° 75 |
| 9 | Décl. calculée moins décl. observée | C − O, ″ |
| 10 | Longitude calculée moins longitude observée | C − O in *geocentric* longitude, ″ |
| 11 | Latitude calculée moins latitude observée | C − O in geocentric latitude, ″ |

- **Frame.** The observed and computed places are apparent geocentric RA and Dec of date.
  - The ephemeris n° 75 (p. 111, §72) is computed from the heliocentric ephemerides "pour le
    minuit moyen du méridien de Paris".
  - It adds nutation N, the aberration Ab (from the diurnal motion and the distance), and
    Bessel's obliquity, to give "l'ascension droite apparente ℞ et la déclinaison apparente D".
- **Longitude and latitude errors are derived, not measured.** δG and δb come from δ℞ and δD
  through P, Q, R, S factors (formulas A and B, p. 127–128; factors from a table published with
  the Greenwich observations of 1836).
  - When several days fall at one epoch, δG for each day is formed from that day's δ℞ and the
    epoch's mean δb. So δG depends almost only on the RA error.
  - This is also how an RA-only observation gets a longitude error, e.g. Bradley 1753, whose
    Dec columns are blank ("» » »").
- **Ancient rows (1690–1771):**
  - 1690 Dec 23; 1712 Apr 2; 1715 Mar 4, Mar 10, Apr 29; 1750 Oct 14, Dec 3; 1753 Dec 3;
    1756 Sep 25; 1764 Jan 15; 1768 Dec 27, 30; 1769 Jan 15, 16, 20, 21, 22, 23; 1771 Dec 18.
  - That is 19 rows, by the observation counts in the table of the 33 conditions (below).
  - Le Verrier re-reduced Flamsteed, Bradley, Mayer and Lemonnier (§74, pp. 125–126; the section number is from the OCR). He took
    Mayer 1756 from Bessel's *Fundamenta* p. 384, and Lemonnier's positions from the
    *Connaissance des Temps pour 1821* with corrected times.
- **Modern rows (1781–1845).**
  - The text (p. 126) says 262 meridian observations were chosen, "soit dans les
    oppositions, soit dans les quadratures".
  - The counts in the 33-condition table sum to 260 modern + 19 ancient = **279**. That matches
    Bertrand's 279 data points. The 262 vs 260 difference is unresolved (see open questions).
- **There is no observer or observatory column.** The observatory follows from the text
  (p. 127):
  - **Greenwich:** 1781–1800; January 1807, 1808 and 1809; and 1828, 1829 and 1830. Taken from
    Airy's *Reduction of the Observations of the Planets from 1750 to 1830*, re-discussed by Le
    Verrier, who notes his numbers differ from Greenwich's in places.
  - **Paris:** 1801–1828, from the *Connaissance des Temps* and two folio volumes. And
    1835–1845, an unpublished series Arago gave him.
  - 1828 is in both ranges.
- **No weights are printed** in table 77.

### 2. §78, pp. 137–141: one mean equation per epoch

"Équations de condition entre les corrections des éléments du mouvement elliptique d'Uranus,
et les erreurs des Tables en longitude."
- Columns: year, date, number of observations grouped, then
  `(α − eH)δε + (α − eH)t·δn + H·eδϖ + K·δe + (long. calc. − long. obs.) = 0`.
- The constant term is the mean geocentric-longitude C − O of the group, e.g. −65.9″ for
  1690 Dec 23 (1 obs) and −14.8″ for 1781 Sep 27 (2 obs).
- These are normal places in geocentric longitude only, against the "théorie admise": his
  Jupiter and Saturn perturbations and his preliminary ellipse.

### 3. §131, p. 222: the 33 conditions used in the perturber fit

"Formation des équations de condition."
- The §78 equations are averaged further into 33 "conditions moyennes", each over a few
  observations close in time:
  - ancient: 1690, 1712–1715, 1750, 1753–1756, 1764, 1768–1769, 1771;
  - modern: then roughly two-year groups from 1781–1782 to 1844–1845.
- Each condition is weighted by its number of observations ("proportionnellement au nombre
  d'observations"). The counts sum to 279.
- The perturbation terms [1]…[33] are added to the same left-hand sides for each trial
  perturber.
- Constant terms run from −65.9″ (1690) through −37.2″ (1811–1813) to +105.65″ (1844–1845).

### What this means for the M6a schema

- One row per table 77 observation: `quantity` = apparent RA and Dec of date, with
  `lv_residual` = his C − O in RA, Dec, longitude and latitude.
- `observatory` is inferred from the p. 127 date ranges, citing that page, not read from the
  row. The 1828 rows need a rule (see open questions).
- `lv_weight` is not printed per observation. The only weights are the group counts of §78 and
  §131.
- The §131 normal places can be a third, derived table. They would let us check our grouping
  against his: same 279 observations, same 33 groups.

## M6a decisions (2026-11-06)

- **What is fitted.** The table n° 77 per-observation rows. The §131 33 normal places are used
  only as a cross-check that reproduces Le Verrier's own fit.
- **Observable.** Apparent geocentric RA and Dec of date, modelled with light-time,
  aberration and nutation on both reduction tracks (1846 and modern).
  - RA-only rows (e.g. Bradley 1753) get a 1-D likelihood.
  - Le Verrier's derived longitude and latitude residuals (columns 10–11) are transcribed but
    not used.
- **Time meridian.** The meridian of "Temps moyen" is recorded per row as an assumption. It
  matters little: the Paris–Greenwich offset of 9 m 21 s is ≈ 0.27″ of Uranus's mean
  heliocentric motion. The apparent geocentric motion is faster near opposition, about
  150″/day or ≈ 1″ over the offset, and near zero at the stationary points. Later
  there is one sensitivity fit with the Greenwich rows shifted by that offset.
- **Transcription.** Every table 77 row is transcribed exactly as printed. The 1828 rows'
  observatory was left `ambiguous` at first. It was later resolved as Greenwich, by inference
  (see the loader section).
- **Double entry.**
  - Pass 1 is from the archive.org offprint and pass 2 from the Lille offprint, written
    independently.
  - The Gallica CdT 1849 scan is the third witness for every disagreement. Each resolution is
    logged with its source page.
  - Page images only; the embedded OCR is not used for tables.
- **No real-data fit** until A6c (synthetic coverage with the M6 observation model) passes.
- **A6b** keeps the equinox/precession loader test; the old A4 numeric targets are dropped
  (PHASE1_SPEC).

## Count discrepancy: 262 vs 260 modern observations

- The text (p. 126) says 262 modern meridian observations were chosen.
- The observation counts in the §131 table (p. 222) sum to 260 modern + 19 ancient = 279. That
  matches Bertrand's (2025) 279 data points.
- **Table 77 has 279 rows: 19 ancient + 260 modern**, counted independently by both passes
  (per page 31, 37, 34, 37, 37, 34, 39, 30). So the table agrees with §131, and the "262" is in
  the text of p. 126 only. Possibly two observations were chosen and then dropped before the
  table was set, or it is a misprint; nothing in the table marks two rejected rows.

## M6a loader: `leverrier_1846.csv` (spec schema)

`uv run python -m fork.data.observations` builds it from `leverrier_1846_table77.csv` and the
misprint table. `tests/test_historical.py` checks it equals that build.

- **One row per measured quantity:** 279 observations give 279 RA rows and 250 Dec rows. An
  observation's rows share `obs_id`. The 29 RA-only observations (Dec printed "» » »") have no
  Dec row, which gives the 1-D likelihood. This reads the spec's "one row per observation" as
  one row per measured quantity, so each row has a single `quantity` / `value` / `unit`.
- **Values** are in degrees (`value`), with the printed string and its units beside them.
  - Ancient rows (before 1781) print RA in arc (° ′ ″), and their computed seconds and C − O in
    ″.
  - Modern rows print RA in time (h m s), and their computed seconds and C − O in s of time.
  - `lv_residual` is O − C (the table prints C − O) in ″ of the coordinate. For modern RA that
    is 15 × the printed seconds, not multiplied by cos δ.
  - A test rebuilds every printed computed-seconds value from `value` and `lv_residual`, which
    checks the units in both eras.
- **1843 "− 0,33. 2,7".** The print uses a comma as the degree separator. The transcription
  normalized it to "-0 33 2.7", following its separator rule. The parser reads the sign from
  the string, so −0° keeps its sign. It accepts a decimal only in the last part, so
  "-0.33 2.7" raises rather than loading as −0.33°. There are test rows for this and for
  the ancient case.
- **Frame and equinox** (p. 111 §72; p. 127): apparent geocentric RA and Dec, true equator and
  equinox of date, with nutation and aberration. `equinox_ref = of_date`, with that citation.
  - §72 states no precession constant, so `precession_constant = not_stated`, with a citation
    saying so. The loader accepts `not_stated` only for `of_date`, where no fixed-epoch
    reduction is undone.
  - A `fixed` record needs a numeric constant and an epoch, and a record missing either field
    fails to load.
  - **Le Verrier's own precession constant is not needed** (decision, 2026-11-06).
    - The modern track models the printed apparent-of-date positions directly: the forward
      model's geocentric Uranus goes to the true equator and equinox of date with IAU 2006
      precession-nutation. No 1846 constant enters.
    - The 1846 track is validated against Le Verrier's own printed computed column instead.
      It reconstructs his calculated RA and Dec seconds, and the residual distribution
      against the printed values is reported (M6b).
- **A6b loader test:** passes. J2000 longitudes at 1690–1846 are converted to of date with IAU
  2006, then pre-reduced to 1800.0 with 50.10″/yr.
  - 50.10″/yr is a placeholder, not a historical constant. It is the value the spec fixes for
    this test (`docs/PHASE1_SPEC.md`, A6b loader test, step 2). It sits 0.14″/yr below IAU
    2006's general precession rate at 1800 (50.244″/yr), so the right and wrong paths
    separate by more than 1″ over 1690–1846. No Table 77 source uses it.
  - The loader recovers J2000 to < 0.01″.
  - Two wrong paths both miss by > 1″: treating the values as of date, and undoing the
    reduction with IAU 2006.
  - Records missing the equinox or the constant raise.
- **Observer and observatory** come from the text, not the table:
  - **§74:** Flamsteed (Greenwich) for 1690, 1712 and 1715; Le Monnier (Paris) for 1750 and
    1764–1771; Bradley (Greenwich) for 1753; Mayer (Göttingen) for 1756.
  - **p. 127:** Greenwich for 1781–1800, January 1807/1808/1809, and 1828–1830; Paris for
    1801–1828 and 1835–1845. 1828 is in both ranges; see the 1828 item below.
  - Modern observers are not named, so that field is blank and `instrument` is blank
    throughout.
- **Time: Paris mean time, astronomical reckoning, for every row.**
  - The day begins at mean noon, and `epoch_ut_jd` = JD(civil noon) + t − Paris longitude.
    `epoch_tt_jd` adds ΔT, using the Espenak–Meeus polynomials (6–14 s; Uranus moves
    < 0.01″ in that time).
  - p. 126 says so for Mayer's row ("temps moyen de Paris").
  - For the rest it comes from the **transit check**. These are meridian observations, so
    the apparent sidereal time at the site must equal the observed RA (`transit_resid_s`).
    - With Paris time, the Greenwich rows fit to 0.9 ± 0.6 s (max 3.3 s, 91 rows) and the
      Paris rows to 0.5 ± 0.8 s (max 3.6 s, apart from one row).
    - With Greenwich time, the Greenwich rows miss by ≈ 563 s, which is 9 m 21 s in
      sidereal time.

    So Le Verrier converted the Greenwich times to Paris mean time. The planned sensitivity
    fit with the Greenwich rows shifted by the Paris–Greenwich offset would test an
    alternative that this check rules out.
  - Limit: for the Paris-range rows, "Paris site, Paris time" and "Greenwich site, Greenwich
    time" differ by only ~1.5 s in this check. For those rows it confirms the time
    reckoning, not the site.
- **1828: resolved as Greenwich, by inference** (decision, 2026-11-06; reading
  confirmed: Greenwich site, Paris time).
  `observatory_basis` records the inference, and the four site/time transit residuals are in
  `notes`:

  | site / time | Jul 19 | Jul 20 |
  |---|---|---|
  | Greenwich / Paris mean time | +1.8 s | +2.4 s |
  | Greenwich / Greenwich mean time | +564.3 s | +564.9 s |
  | Paris / Paris mean time | +562.7 s | +563.3 s |
  | Paris / Greenwich mean time | +1125.2 s | +1125.8 s |

  - Only "Greenwich site, Paris mean time" fits. So these two rows are Greenwich observations
    whose times Le Verrier converted to Paris mean time, the same as every other Greenwich
    row. The 1829 Jul 26 row, Greenwich by p. 127, gives the same pattern (+1.9 / +564.4 /
    +562.8 / +1125.3 s).
  - The decision note asked to record that Le Verrier "apparently didn't convert these two to
    Paris time". The numbers don't support that. An unconverted Greenwich time would fit
    "Greenwich site, Greenwich time", and that combination misses by 564 s. The ~563 s miss
    is against the Paris *site*, not against Paris *time*. What the rows show is that they
    were observed at Greenwich, with times converted to Paris like the rest.
  - Airy's *Reduction* would confirm the site, but it isn't needed for the fit.
- **The transit check found three timing discrepancies** in the print. All three are
  verified as printed in Gallica and entered in the misprint table, with reasons that don't
  use the Uranus ephemeris:
  - **1804 Mar 31, apply:** 12 18 13 → 12 17 13. Approved 2026-11-06.
  - **1807 Apr 25, apply:** 11 32 34 → 11 32 54. Approved 2026-11-06.

    The basis for both is the transit identity LST = RA (Earth rotation only), with the
    daily step of the neighbouring mean times as a second, independent check. No ephemeris
    is used.
  - **1768 Dec 30, log only:** off by +9.5 s; the cause was not found.

  Each moves Uranus by < 0.05″, so they don't matter for the fit, but they are recorded.

## M6b: observation model and error calibration

**Observation model** (`fork.model.apparent`, `fork.model.observation`). The modern track
models the printed places directly:
- the integrated Uranus state, corrected for light time (r − τv);
- annual aberration from the Earth's Horizons velocity;
- ecl_j2000 → ICRS → true equator and equinox of date with `erfa.pnm06a` (IAU 2006/2000A).

No 1846 constant enters.
- **Against astropy** (ICRS → TETE, which also includes solar light deflection): agrees to
  0.032″ max over 1690–1845.
- **Whole chain against Horizons:** the integrator followed by the apparent place, with θ =
  the true 1781 states and a 3-D Neptune, matches to < 0.05″ in every group.
- **Not modelled:** light deflection (milliarcseconds), diurnal parallax (the printed places
  are geocentric), and the Horizons/ICRS frame tie (~0.02″).

**Perturber: inclined** (decision, 2026-11-06; scope note in M5.md).
- The planar perturber misfits Dec by 1.9″ rms in 1835–1845. Neptune's mutual inclination
  of 1.5° tilts Uranus's orbit.
- θ gains the perturber's i and Ω relative to Uranus's plane. They are sampled as
  (sin i cos Ω, sin i sin Ω), with prior i ≤ 10° uniform in cos i and Ω uniform.
- With this model the chain matches Horizons to 0.032″ max.
- The Le Verrier shape model stays planar.

**Measurements and residuals.**
- One RA or Dec per measurement: 529 in all, with the 29 RA-only epochs giving RA alone.
- Residuals are on the sky: Δα·cos δ, and Δδ.

**Error groups:** observer series × quantity, with a minimum of 10 per group.
- Table 77 names no instruments. The ancient sightings make one group: Le Monnier has 12,
  but Flamsteed, Bradley and Mayer together have only 7 RA and 6 Dec.
- Modern groups are the p. 127 series: Greenwich 1781–1830, Paris 1801–1827 and Paris
  1835–1845.
- **Likelihood:** Gaussian, with σ_g fixed and the group offsets b_g ~ N(0, τ_g²)
  marginalised analytically.

**Calibration** (`uv run python -m fork.data.calibration` → `data/historical/m6_error_model.json`).
- O − JPL: observed minus the real Uranus's apparent place at the same epoch. This uses

### Why A6c's intervals are narrower than M5b's (5.1° against 9.1°)

**Method:** a local Fisher ablation at the true θ, with no new sampling
(`uv run python docs/notes/scripts/a6c_width_attribution.py`, about 4 s).
- For each configuration, take the Jacobian of the whitened residuals with respect to the free
  parameters at the truth.
- The delta method then gives σ of the 1846 longitude: σ_λ = √(gᵀF⁻¹g).
- The Jacobian depends only on the layout, σ_g, τ_g and the model, not on the data values.
- **Caveat:** this is the curvature at one point and ignores the priors. The priors truncate
  the mass–distance direction, so the Fisher widths come out larger than the posterior's: 2σ
  is 20.5° for M5b and 7.6° for A6c, against posterior widths of 9.1° and 5.1°.
- The Fisher ratio between the two (2.71×) is larger than the posterior ratio (1.78×). The
  prior cuts M5b's wider likelihood more. The attribution below is of the likelihood's
  information, so treat the shares as approximate.

**Path from M5b's setup to A6c's.** Each step adds one change; the shares are of ln(2.71).

| step | σ_λ (°) | factor | share |
|---|---|---|---|
| M5b: 87 heliocentric longitudes (22 at 12″, 65 annual at 3″), 9 parameters | 10.27 | | |
| 1. Table 77's layout: 279 RA (19 ancient, 260 modern), M5b's 12″/3″, no offsets | 5.89 | 1.74× | 56 % |
| 2. modern σ_g calibrated (1.4–2.9″ instead of 3″) | 4.37 | 1.35× | 30 % |
| 3. ancient σ_g calibrated (4.7–5.1″ instead of 12″) | 3.41 | 1.28× | 25 % |
| 4. group offsets marginalised, τ 5″/2″ | 4.01 | 0.85× | −16 % |
| 5. add the 250 Dec measurements | 3.66 | 1.10× | +9 % |
| 6. free i and Ω (11 parameters) = A6c | 3.78 | 0.97× | −3 % |

**Which dominates.** Two contributors are about equal, and together they account for the whole
narrowing.
- **Number of measurements (step 1, 56 %).** Table 77 has 260 individual modern RAs where M5b
  had 65 annual places. Count alone predicts √(260/65) = 2.0×; the step gives 1.74×. The step
  also folds in the real dates (clustered, not one a year) and the change from heliocentric
  longitude to geocentric RA.
- **Per-measurement σ (steps 2–3, 55 % together).** The calibrated σ_g is smaller than M5b's
  assumed noise for the modern groups (1.4–2.9″ against 3″) and much smaller for the ancient
  ones (4.7–5.1″ against 12″).
  - Σ 1/σ² is 7.4″⁻² for M5b and 69″⁻² for A6c's RA alone (140″⁻² with Dec).
  - Swapping only σ, in the full A6c model, back to 12″/3″ widens σ_λ from 3.78° to 6.58°
    (1.74×), which agrees with steps 2–3 combined (1.35 × 1.28 = 1.73).

**The rest are minor:**
- **Group offsets** cost 16 %. With τ → 0, σ_λ is 3.27°; at the pre-registered τ it is 3.78°;
  with τ → ∞ it is 3.88°.
- **Dec** adds 9 % once the offsets are in.
  - Without offsets Dec would add 1.56×, because an absolute Dec then pins the ancient-versus-
    modern difference. The offsets remove most of that.
  - Dec's real job is the inclination. With i and Ω free and RA only, σ_λ is 11.3°: RA cannot
    constrain the out-of-plane pull. Adding Dec brings it back to 3.8°.
- **The two extra parameters** cost 3 %.

**The ancient sightings are essential.** Without them, σ_λ is 32° even with every modern RA and
Dec. The 1690–1771 baseline is what fixes the perturber's longitude, as in M4/M5.

**Merged posteriors** (`dynesty.utils.merge_runs`), three of them:
- **R2 only** (r2_1 + r2_2). This is the replacement candidate.
- **R1 only** (r1_1–r1_4) and **R1 + R2** (all six). These are reported for transparency and
  never enter the reported interval. R1 is diagnostic.

For each merge:
- Its own median, width and ln Z.
- **Its interval:** the headline's cells 0, 1 and 3 with the merged cell 2 in place of the
  headline's (cell shares from the merged ln Z). It is summarised as `m6c.summarize_variant`
  does: 6000 derived samples, rng `[7, 1846, 6, 10]`.

**Decision rule (stated now).** Two conditions:
- (a) **R2 is consistent:** its two 68 % widths differ by at most 10 % of their mean.
- (b) **R1's spread exceeds dynesty's error:** χ² of the four R1 widths about their mean, with
  each run's resample error, has p < 0.01 (χ²₃ > 11.34).

| (a) | (b) | outcome | reported interval |
|---|---|---|---|
| holds | holds | `replace` | The headline's cells 0, 1 and 3 with **the merge of R2's runs only** as cell 2. R1 is not merged into it. |
| holds | fails | `cross_check` | The headline as published, with R1's run-to-run std of the median and of the width beside it as a systematic. **R2's merged width is shown beside it as a cross-check, not a replacement.** |
| fails | either | `systematic_only` | The headline as published, with R1's run-to-run std of the median and of the width beside it as a systematic. |

- A systematic is never folded into the interval.
- All three merges (R2, R1, R1 + R2) and their intervals are computed and reported in every
  outcome.
- `site/data/m6_fits.json` is not edited; its headline record stays as first published. The
  study writes `site/data/m6c_repeat.json`:
  - `decision.outcome` holds the outcome.
  - `reported` holds the interval the rule selects, and in the `cross_check` outcome also
    `cross_check_not_replacement`.
  - `merges` holds all three merges.
- The S1–S3 numbers are not revised by this study.

**Thresholds, and what they mean:**
- 10 % is about 0.4°–0.6° on widths of 4°–6°. That is a quarter of the 2.4° gap that prompted
  the study.
- If R1 scatters no more than its errors, the headline/S1 gap was a rare event, and the
  headline stands with the spread as a systematic.
- If R2 disagrees with itself, stronger exploration has not been shown to converge, and the
  same applies.
- R1 is not merged into the replacement because R1 runs at the settings whose
  reproducibility is in question. A merge would carry in whatever regions the R1 runs
  under-sample.

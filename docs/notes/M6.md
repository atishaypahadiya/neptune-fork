# M6: Historical data

## M6a stop condition: what Le Verrier's memoir tabulates

Source read: Le Verrier, *Recherches sur les mouvements de la planète Herschel (dite Uranus)*,
Paris, Bachelier, 1846. This is the offprint of *Connaissance des Temps pour 1849*, Additions,
pp. 3–254. Scan used: archive.org `bub_gb_HMi0NyDVkhQC`, a Google Books copy with 264 PDF
pages, page images 1608 × 2500 px:
https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_HMi0NyDVkhQC. Printed page *p* is PDF page *p* + 6 (index
*p* + 5) in the table pages checked.

**Answer: the memoir tabulates observed positions, one row per observation, with Le Verrier's
O − C beside them.** So positions can be recovered without his tables, and the source plan
stands. There are three levels.

### 1. Table n° 77, pp. 129–136: individual observations

"Tableau de la comparaison des observations d'Uranus avec la théorie admise." The 11 columns,
as printed (described in the text, p. 127):

| # | column | notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Années | year |
| 2 | Mois et jours | civil date |
| 3 | Temps moyen | mean time of the observation, h m s. The meridian is not stated in the table (see open questions) |
| 4 | Ascension droite observée | modern rows in time (h m s, to 0.01 s); ancient rows in arc (° ′ ″, to 0.1″) |
| 5 | Secondes de l'AR calculée | seconds only, from the geocentric ephemeris n° 75 |
| 6 | AR calculée moins AR observée | C − O, s (modern) or ″ (ancient) |
| 7 | Déclinaison observée | ° ′ ″, to 0.1″ |
| 8 | Secondes de la déclinaison calculée | from n° 75 |
| 9 | Décl. calculée moins décl. observée | C − O, ″ |
| 10 | Longitude calculée moins longitude observée | C − O in *geocentric* longitude, ″ |
| 11 | Latitude calculée moins latitude observée | C − O in geocentric latitude, ″ |

- **Frame.** The observed and computed places are apparent geocentric RA and Dec of date.
  - The ephemeris n° 75 (p. 111, §72) is computed from the heliocentric ephemerides "pour le
    minuit moyen du méridien de Paris".
  - It adds nutation N, the aberration Ab (from the diurnal motion and the distance), and
    Bessel's obliquity, to give "l'ascension droite apparente ℞ et la déclinaison apparente D".
- **Longitude and latitude errors are derived, not measured.** δG and δb come from δ℞ and δD
  through P, Q, R, S factors (formulas A and B, p. 127–128; factors from a table published with
  the Greenwich observations of 1836).
  - When several days fall at one epoch, δG for each day is formed from that day's δ℞ and the
    epoch's mean δb. So δG depends almost only on the RA error.
  - This is also how an RA-only observation gets a longitude error, e.g. Bradley 1753, whose
    Dec columns are blank ("» » »").
- **Ancient rows (1690–1771):**
  - 1690 Dec 23; 1712 Apr 2; 1715 Mar 4, Mar 10, Apr 29; 1750 Oct 14, Dec 3; 1753 Dec 3;
    1756 Sep 25; 1764 Jan 15; 1768 Dec 27, 30; 1769 Jan 15, 16, 20, 21, 22, 23; 1771 Dec 18.
  - That is 19 rows, by the observation counts in the table of the 33 conditions (below).
  - Le Verrier re-reduced Flamsteed, Bradley, Mayer and Lemonnier (§74, pp. 125–126; the section number is from the OCR). He took
    Mayer 1756 from Bessel's *Fundamenta* p. 384, and Lemonnier's positions from the
    *Connaissance des Temps pour 1821* with corrected times.
- **Modern rows (1781–1845).**
  - The text (p. 126) says 262 meridian observations were chosen, "soit dans les
    oppositions, soit dans les quadratures".
  - The counts in the 33-condition table sum to 260 modern + 19 ancient = **279**. That matches
    Bertrand's 279 data points. The 262 vs 260 difference is unresolved (see open questions).
- **There is no observer or observatory column.** The observatory follows from the text
  (p. 127):
  - **Greenwich:** 1781–1800; January 1807, 1808 and 1809; and 1828, 1829 and 1830. Taken from
    Airy's *Reduction of the Observations of the Planets from 1750 to 1830*, re-discussed by Le
    Verrier, who notes his numbers differ from Greenwich's in places.
  - **Paris:** 1801–1828, from the *Connaissance des Temps* and two folio volumes. And
    1835–1845, an unpublished series Arago gave him.
  - 1828 is in both ranges.
- **No weights are printed** in table 77.

### 2. §78, pp. 137–141: one mean equation per epoch

"Équations de condition entre les corrections des éléments du mouvement elliptique d'Uranus,
et les erreurs des Tables en longitude."
- Columns: year, date, number of observations grouped, then
  `(α − eH)δε + (α − eH)t·δn + H·eδϖ + K·δe + (long. calc. − long. obs.) = 0`.
- The constant term is the mean geocentric-longitude C − O of the group, e.g. −65.9″ for
  1690 Dec 23 (1 obs) and −14.8″ for 1781 Sep 27 (2 obs).
- These are normal places in geocentric longitude only, against the "théorie admise": his
  Jupiter and Saturn perturbations and his preliminary ellipse.

### 3. §131, p. 222: the 33 conditions used in the perturber fit

"Formation des équations de condition."
- The §78 equations are averaged further into 33 "conditions moyennes", each over a few
  observations close in time:
  - ancient: 1690, 1712–1715, 1750, 1753–1756, 1764, 1768–1769, 1771;
  - modern: then roughly two-year groups from 1781–1782 to 1844–1845.
- Each condition is weighted by its number of observations ("proportionnellement au nombre
  d'observations"). The counts sum to 279.
- The perturbation terms [1]…[33] are added to the same left-hand sides for each trial
  perturber.
- Constant terms run from −65.9″ (1690) through −37.2″ (1811–1813) to +105.65″ (1844–1845).

### What this means for the M6a schema

- One row per table 77 observation: `quantity` = apparent RA and Dec of date, with
  `lv_residual` = his C − O in RA, Dec, longitude and latitude.
- `observatory` is inferred from the p. 127 date ranges, citing that page, not read from the
  row. The 1828 rows need a rule (see open questions).
- `lv_weight` is not printed per observation. The only weights are the group counts of §78 and
  §131.
- The §131 normal places can be a third, derived table. They would let us check our grouping
  against his: same 279 observations, same 33 groups.

   which two are dropped or merged by §131.

## M6a decisions (user, 2026-11-06)

- **What is fitted.** The table n° 77 per-observation rows. The §131 33 normal places are used
  only as a cross-check that reproduces Le Verrier's own fit.
- **Observable.** Apparent geocentric RA and Dec of date, modelled with light-time,
  aberration and nutation on both reduction tracks (1846 and modern).
  - RA-only rows (e.g. Bradley 1753) get a 1-D likelihood.
  - Le Verrier's derived longitude and latitude residuals (columns 10–11) are transcribed but
    not used.
- **Time meridian.** The meridian of "Temps moyen" is recorded per row as an assumption. It
  matters little: the Paris–Greenwich offset of 9 m 21 s is ≈ 0.27″ of Uranus's mean
  heliocentric motion. The apparent geocentric motion is faster near opposition, about
  150″/day or ≈ 1″ over the offset, and near zero at the stationary points. Later
  there is one sensitivity fit with the Greenwich rows shifted by that offset.
- **Transcription.** Every table 77 row is transcribed exactly as printed. The 1828 rows'
  observatory is `ambiguous` until Airy's *Reduction* (deferred) resolves it.
- **Double entry.**
  - Pass 1 is from the archive.org offprint and pass 2 from the Lille offprint, written
    independently.
  - The Gallica CdT 1849 scan is the third witness for every disagreement. Each resolution is
    logged with its source page.
  - Page images only; the embedded OCR is not used for tables.
- **No real-data fit** until A6c (synthetic coverage with the M6 observation model) passes.
- **A6b** keeps the equinox/precession loader test; the old A4 numeric targets are dropped
  (PHASE1_SPEC).

## Side note: "♆ Le Verrier" in CdT 1849

The symbol table in the front matter of *Connaissance des Temps pour 1849* lists the new
planet as "♆ Le Verrier". This is a primary source for France's attempt to name the planet
after him. It is on PDF p. 2 of the front-matter download from Gallica; the user reported it,
and I have not checked it against a scan here.

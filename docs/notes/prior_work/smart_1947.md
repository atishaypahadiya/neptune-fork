## Claims

1. The *Nature* article opens by setting the discovery of Neptune apart from the telescopic
   discoveries of Uranus and the minor planets: the discrepancies of Uranus "furnished the
   means" by which Adams and Le Verrier "deduce[d] independently the position of a new planet".
   [read: landing-page description, first paragraph only]
2. The Occasional Notes paper is said to be the fullest account of Adams's work from his
   manuscripts. [not read: `docs/notes/P1B_adams_reproduction.md` § 0, which itself had not
   obtained it]
3. Smart is said to argue (p. 55) that Adams's second hypothesis had "a small advantage over
   the first". [not read: Kollerstrom 2006, Part 1, note 126, read as OCR]
4. Smart is said to represent "the traditional view" of Adams's undated papers, and is quoted
   for the phrase "completely destitute of but the barest recognition of Adams's achievement".
   [not read: Kollerstrom 2006, Part 2, pp. 357 and 364, OCR; the sentences around the
   quotations were only skimmed]
5. Smart suggested to Brookes the problem of re-deriving Adams's solutions. [read: Brookes
   1970, p. 80, acknowledgements]

## What it means for this repository

`P1B_adams_reproduction.md` § 0 and R10 already say that Smart was sought and not obtained, and
limit the novelty claim of the Adams reproduction accordingly. Nothing here changes that. If
Smart tabulates a comparison of Hypotheses I and II against Neptune (claim 3 suggests he judges
between them), it would overlap with the reproduction's Q1 tables; I cannot say whether he
recomputes anything.

## Needs a browser / library

- The *Nature* article, pp. 648–652: needs a subscription or a library.
- The Occasional Notes pamphlet (vol. 2, no. 11, 1947): print only as far as I could find; a

  lists. To be read for: any recomputation of Adams's solutions, the judgement between the two

# M4: Forward model and synthetic data (A2)

## Built
- `fork/model/forward.py`: generic interface `model.param_names` / `model.predict(θ, t_jd)`.
  - `UranusModel` has 6 parameters; `predict_uranus_lon(θ, t_jd, context)` accepts 6 (Uranus) or
    11 (Uranus + planar perturber) entries.
  - The numba kernel converts elements to states and integrates Sun + Jupiter + Saturn + Uranus
    (+ perturber) with WH at the defaults (dt = 14 d, REBOUND composition). It returns the
    heliocentric longitude `lon_ecl_j2000_rad`.
  - `ForwardContext` holds the epoch (1781-03-13) and the unfitted Jupiter/Saturn ICs.
  - Uranus θ = (a, e, i, Ω, ϖ, λ₀): fits free a, e, ϖ, λ₀ and hold i, Ω at epoch values (rule 2).
    Perturber θ = (log10 m [M⊕], a, e, ϖ, λ₀), planar.
- `fork/data/synthetic.py`: truth is the Horizons Sun-centred Uranus longitude (which includes
  Neptune). The schedule is 22 pre-discovery sightings 1690–1771 at σ = 12″ and 65 annual
  epochs 1781–1845 at σ = 3″.
  - The years and counts approximate the commonly cited record (Flamsteed 6, Bradley 3, Mayer 1,
    Le Monnier 12). They're placed at Jan 1.0 TDB as a stand-in until M6 brings real dates.
  - Seeded through an explicit `rng`. `LongitudeObservations` never carries the truth.
- `fork/inference/fit.py`: `fit_uranus_only`, a Levenberg–Marquardt fit of a, e, ϖ, λ₀ with i, Ω
  held (`fork.model.forward.uranus_inc_raan_at_epoch`).
  - It works in non-singular variables (a, e·cos ϖ, e·sin ϖ, λ₀, tan(i/2)·cos Ω, tan(i/2)·sin Ω).
  - The start is **data-only**: mean motion from the post-1781 longitudes, then Kepler III, with
    e = 0. Horizons Uranus is never used.
  - Diagnostic `free_inc_raan=True` also fits i, Ω, starting from i = 1° with
    Ω ∈ {0, 45, 90, 135}° and keeping the best (the gradient vanishes at i = 0).
- `fork/data/horizons.py`: `inner_barycenter_state_ecl_j2000`,
  `states_about_inner_barycenter_ecl_j2000`. `bodies.py` gains the inner planets (Horizons
  199, 299, 3, 4).
- `tests/data/horizons_m4.json` (24 KB, public-domain JPL) and a module-scoped `offline_horizons`
  fixture, so the M4 tests run in CI without network. Any attempted fetch fails loudly.
- `docs/notes/M4_residuals.png` (script: `docs/notes/scripts/m4_residuals_plot.py`).

## Big surprise: the inner planets must be folded in at their barycenter
Before this fix, integrating the **raw Horizons states** of Jupiter–Neptune (full 3-D, WH and RK4
agreeing to 0.01″) missed Horizons Uranus by **±35″** over 1690–1846, and Neptune by ±31″.
- Cause: the inner planets' mass is folded into the central body, so that body must sit at the
  **Sun + inner-planet barycenter**. Horizons states are relative to the Sun's centre, which moves
  around that barycenter: 2.8e-6 AU and **7.4 cm/s** at 1781.
- That velocity offset is a 1.3e-5 relative error in Uranus's velocity. It shifts the mean motion
  and accumulates secularly.
- Re-centring the ICs on the inner barycenter: Uranus **0.09″** max, 0.04″ RMS; Neptune 0.07″.
- Through the full forward model, planting the planar real Neptune now reproduces Horizons Uranus
  to **0.55″ max, 0.12″ RMS**. The remainder is mostly Neptune's dropped 1.8° inclination.
- Model outputs are positions about the inner barycenter. They differ from heliocentric by ≤ 3e-6
  AU, i.e. ≤ 0.03″ in Uranus's longitude, so they're compared with heliocentric observations
  directly.
- A regression test pins this. A1 is unaffected: it compares two integrators on the same
  Sun-centred ICs.

## Rule 2: i and Ω are held at their epoch values (review decision 1)
The first M4 pass freed all six Uranus elements, following the original wording of CLAUDE.md
rule 2. On review, rule 2 was corrected: a, e, ϖ, λ₀ are always free, and i, Ω are held at their
osculating 1781 values from Horizons. The spec's M4 line is back to its original text.

Why:
- From longitudes alone, (i, Ω) enter only through the reduction to the ecliptic,
  ≈ −tan²(i/2)·sin 2u (about 9″ for Uranus). That's a spare second harmonic, invariant under
  Ω → Ω + 180°.
- Freed, it soaks up Neptune's signal. In the no-perturber fit, χ²/dof drops from 21.6 (held)
  to 6.5 (free), and i, Ω wander to 0.86–0.89°, 147–150° (true values 0.777°, 73.8°).
- On Neptune-free data generated by the model itself, the free mode recovers i, Ω exactly
  (`test_free_inc_raan_diagnostic_recovers_model_truth`). It survives as `free_inc_raan=True`,
  for diagnostics only.

**The leak, quantified** (`tests/test_forward.py::test_inc_raan_leak`):
- Method: integrate J+S+U with and without Neptune from the same 1781 ICs, sampling every 30 d
  through 1846. Neptune's effect on Uranus's osculating elements is |Δi| ≤ **2.4″** and
  |ΔΩ| ≤ **64″**.
- Conversion to longitude: at each time, take the worst case over all orbital longitudes of the
  change in ecliptic longitude that (Δi, ΔΩ) causes.
- Result: **0.017″** (largest at 1846), against the 1″ budget. Holding i, Ω at 1781 values
  carries essentially none of Neptune's signal into the fit.
- For scale, Jupiter and Saturn alone move Uranus's osculating i by 12″ and Ω by ~1100″ over the
  same span. The model integrates those changes; only the epoch values are held.

## Caveats on the A2 criteria
- The residual curve has **six** slope sign changes over 1690–1845, not one. Most of the
  large-scale structure (+38″ at 1690, −105″ at 1738, +33″ at 1770) sits in the sparse
  pre-discovery era. The post-1781 part is the familiar Le Verrier signature: small wiggles, a
  turnover around 1818–1833, then Uranus falling behind (−20″ by 1845). "A sign change within
  1815–1830" is satisfied, but it is weak evidence on its own.
- The peak-to-peak is dominated by the extrapolated gaps (1690–1712 and 1715–1748), so it depends
  on the pre-discovery epoch schedule. Revisit with the real M6 dates.
